# Validation Runner

## Purpose

This runner validates US law-firm leads for the Service-Page Gap campaign and writes the first outreach email.

The job is **not** to find “a missing page.”

The job is to understand how the firm presents its main services, decide whether different legal clients are being forced to share the same general website conversation when one or more services deserve their own client-specific experience, select the strongest service as the first mockup example, and only then locate a direct public email for the relevant decision maker.

> **The gap is not “this service is missing a page.” The gap is that different legal clients are being asked to share the same conversation. The service we choose is simply the best first example.**

---

## Non-Negotiable Rules

1. Work only with services the firm **explicitly says it handles**.
2. Do not infer a practice area from attorney background, credentials, or general capability.
3. Never use search-engine results alone to prove that a dedicated page does not exist.
4. Do not hunt for tiny subservice gaps when a substantial main-service page already exists.
5. Do not imply that only the selected service deserves a dedicated page.
6. Client experience comes first. Google and AI are secondary supporting benefits.
7. Do not make ranking guarantees or indexing-time promises.
8. If an important validation point is unclear, mark the lead **Review**, not Qualified.
9. **Email research happens last, only after the service-page opportunity has passed validation.**
10. A lead cannot become Qualified without a **direct public decision-maker email**.
11. Generic inboxes are not acceptable: no `info@`, `contact@`, `office@`, `reception@`, `admin@`, `support@`, `hello@`, `team@`, or equivalent role inboxes.
12. Never guess, synthesize, or infer an email address from a naming pattern. Use only a publicly available address you can actually source.
13. Prefer the decision maker’s personal professional email. A named business email for that individual is also acceptable if public and attributable to them.
14. Record evidence for every material claim.

# Workflow

## Step 0 — Load the Lead

For each lead, start with:

- Firm name
- Firm domain
- Existing source / Place ID if available
- Known city/state if available
- Current internal lead status
- Any prior validation notes

Do not rely on prior notes as proof. Re-check the live website.

---

## Step 1 — Understand the Firm Before Looking for a Gap

Inspect the actual website.

At minimum check:

- Homepage
- Main navigation
- Services / Practice Areas page
- Relevant service sections
- Footer links
- “Learn More” / “Read More” / service buttons
- Attorney / About pages if they mention practice focus
- Internal links
- Sitemap when useful

Search-engine results may be used to discover possible pages, but the website itself is the source of truth for whether a focused service destination exists.

### Capture

Record:

- Main services explicitly offered
- Where each service appears
- Whether major services share one broad page
- Whether some services already have dedicated pages
- Any service buttons that lead back to a general page
- Any structural pattern that helps explain the opportunity

---

## Step 2 — Identify Meaningful Main-Service Opportunities

A strong gap usually looks like this:

> The firm clearly offers several important legal services, but one or more main services are explained only inside the homepage, a general Services page, a broad Practice Areas page, or a one-page website.

A page counts as dedicated when the page itself is overwhelmingly about that service, even if the URL is generic.

Example:

`/legal-services`

can count as a dedicated Family Law page if practically the whole page is about Family Law.

### Strong opportunities

- A major service is only a section on a general Practice Areas page.
- A major service appears on the homepage but has no focused destination.
- A “Learn More” button for the service leads to a general page.
- Two major services have dedicated pages but another equally important service does not.
- A service already has useful content that could naturally become a focused client journey.
- A service serves a materially different type of client from the rest of the shared page.

### Reject micro-gaps

Do **not** qualify primarily on gaps like:

- Child Support when there is already a strong Family Law page
- Green Cards when there is already a strong Immigration page
- Wills when there is already a substantial Estate Planning page

Those may be valid in another campaign. They are not the primary target here.

---

## Step 3 — Think in Client Conversations

Before choosing a mockup service, answer:

> Who is the person looking for this service, and what are they likely trying to understand when they arrive?

Keep this grounded and practical.

Examples:

- **Estate Planning:** planning ahead, protection, clarity, family, peace of mind
- **Probate:** what happens next, process, documents, responsibilities, practical next steps after a death
- **Family Law:** personal situation, sensitivity, clarity, direct answers, next steps
- **Business / Corporate:** practical decisions, contracts, structure, risk, ongoing counsel
- **Immigration:** whether the attorney handles the person’s situation and what path may be available

Do not write amateur psychology. The point is simply that different legal matters need different conversations.

A dedicated page may therefore differ in:

- Message
- Information hierarchy
- Imagery
- Layout
- CTA
- Tone
- Visual mood

while remaining inside the firm’s overall brand.

---

## Step 4 — Choose the Best First Mockup Service

The chosen service is the **first example**, not necessarily the only service that could benefit from a focused page.

Choose the service that makes the clearest, strongest, easiest case.

Good reasons:

- It already has useful content.
- Its client journey is clearly different from the other services.
- Other major services already have dedicated pages but this one does not.
- It is central to the firm’s positioning.
- A relevant attorney credential makes the service especially interesting.
- A “Learn More” button exists but does not lead to a focused destination.
- It is visually or structurally easy to demonstrate as a standalone client experience.

---

## Step 5 — Write the Internal Validation Note Before the Email

Do not write outreach copy until these notes are complete.

Use this format:

**Service chosen for mockup:**  
Business Law

**Evidence:**  
Explicitly offered on `/practice-areas`; no focused Business Law destination found after checking navigation, service links, internal links and sitemap/search discovery.

**Why this is a good example:**  
Business clients have a materially different journey from the family/estate clients currently sharing the same general page.

**Email angle:**  
Separate client conversations; Business Law used as the first example rather than presented as the only structural opportunity.

**Validation:**  
Qualified / Review / Reject

### Required proof

For a Qualified or Review decision, retain:

- Page URLs checked
- Short evidence notes
- The exact service wording used by the firm
- Why the existing destination is not reasonably dedicated
- Why the selected service is a meaningful main service
- Why the client conversation is materially different

---

## Step 6 — Validation Gate

Before proceeding to email research, answer all of these:

1. Is the service explicitly offered?  
   **Must be Yes.**

2. Did I inspect the current website for a dedicated page?  
   **Must be Yes.**

3. Would a reasonable person consider the existing page dedicated to that service?  
   **Must be No.**

4. Am I targeting a meaningful main service rather than a tiny subservice?  
   **Must be Yes.**

5. Can I explain why dedicated pages make sense for this firm without pretending the selected service is the only one that deserves one?  
   **Must be Yes.**

6. Is there a genuine personalized observation from the live site?  
   **Must be Yes.**

7. Can the email explain the client experience before Google/AI?  
   **Must be Yes.**

8. Can I avoid ranking/indexing guarantees?  
   **Must be Yes.**

If any important answer is unclear:

**Validation = Review**

Do not start email hunting for a Review lead unless explicitly instructed to do so.

---

# Step 7 — Identify the Decision Maker

Only after the lead passes the service-page validation gate.

Prefer people who can realistically approve marketing/web work.

Typical priority:

1. Owner / Founder
2. Managing Partner
3. Named Partner
4. Managing Attorney
5. Solo Attorney / Principal
6. Other clearly senior decision maker

Use the firm’s About, Team, Attorneys, Leadership, footer, contact information, and public professional profiles to identify the person.

Record:

- Full name
- Title
- Evidence URL(s)
- Why this person is the likely decision maker

If the firm is too large for a partner to be the realistic web/marketing buyer and there is a clearly named marketing/business-development decision maker, mark for **Review** unless campaign instructions explicitly allow that role.

---

# Step 8 — Find a Direct Public Decision-Maker Email

This happens **last**.

Search both:

### On-site

- Attorney bio page
- Contact page
- Footer
- Team page
- PDF bios / downloadable vCards
- HTML source only when appropriate and public
- Other publicly linked firm assets

### Off-site

Public sources are allowed when needed, for example:

- Public professional profiles
- State/local bar profiles when they publish the attorney’s email
- Public conference/speaker bios
- Public association/directories
- Public firm listings
- Public PDFs
- Other credible pages clearly attributable to the named decision maker

### Acceptable

- `jane@firm.com`
- `jane.doe@firm.com`
- A named professional address directly associated with the decision maker
- A public personal professional email used by that attorney for business

### Not acceptable

- `info@firm.com`
- `contact@firm.com`
- `reception@firm.com`
- `office@firm.com`
- `admin@firm.com`
- `support@firm.com`
- generic form-only contact with no direct email
- guessed email patterns
- emails from data leaks, private databases, or non-public sources

### Email evidence record

Save:

- Decision maker name
- Title
- Email
- Source URL
- Source type: on-site / off-site
- Exact evidence note showing the address belongs to that person

If no direct public decision-maker email can be found:

**Validation outcome = Not Qualified — No Direct Decision-Maker Email**

The service-page gap may be valid, but the lead is not outreach-ready for this campaign.

---

# Step 9 — Write the Email

Target length: **130–160 words**.

A little longer is allowed only when personalization genuinely earns it.

## Order

### A. Personalized observation

The first line must prove the website was actually inspected.

Good:

> I noticed Estate Planning, Probate & Heirship, Business and Adoptions all have to share the same main site experience.

Good:

> Your Probate & Estate Administration section caught my attention because you already explain that work in real detail.

Bad:

> I recently came across your impressive website.

No generic compliments.

### B. Broader insight

Explain that the services may belong to one firm, but the people looking for them are often in very different situations.

Use only 2–3 examples.

### C. Why dedicated pages make sense

Core idea:

> Dedicated pages let each service have the right conversation with the person looking for it instead of asking one general page to do everything.

### D. Three short benefits

Keep them scan-friendly:

- speak directly to that type of client
- give Google a focused page to crawl and index
- give AI tools clearer context about that service

Do not promise rankings.

Do not promise indexing within a fixed period.

### E. Why this service was chosen as the mockup

Example:

> Your Corporate section already has a good amount of substance, so I’m using that as the first mockup.

This makes clear that the selected service is the first example, not the only service that could benefit.

### F. CTA

Use the default soft CTA:

> I’m putting together a quick mockup to show you what I mean. I’ll send it over in the next couple of days.
>
> If you’d rather I didn’t, just let me know.

No call-booking request in the first email.

No hard sell.

---

# Step 10 — Subject Line

The subject should sound like a person referring to something they noticed.

Good:

- Your Probate section caught my eye
- A thought on your service pages
- Your Corporate section gave me an idea
- One thing about how the site is organized
- A thought on your practice pages

Avoid:

- SEO Opportunity
- Website Audit Results
- You Are Missing This Page
- Increase Your Google Rankings

Natural is more important than artificially unique.

# Quality-Control Checklist

A lead can be **Qualified** only when all are true:

- [ ] Service is explicitly offered.
- [ ] Live website was inspected.
- [ ] Search results were not used alone to prove absence.
- [ ] No reasonable dedicated page exists for the selected main service.
- [ ] Selected service is meaningful, not a micro-subservice.
- [ ] The opportunity is framed as different client conversations, not “one missing URL.”
- [ ] Selected service is clearly positioned as the first example.
- [ ] Client experience is explained before Google/AI.
- [ ] No SEO ranking/indexing guarantees are made.
- [ ] First email sentence is based on a genuine observation.
- [ ] Decision maker is identified and evidenced.
- [ ] A direct public email for that decision maker exists.
- [ ] Email is not generic.
- [ ] Email was not guessed.
- [ ] Source URL for the email is saved.
- [ ] Outreach email is concise and personalized.

---

# Anti-Patterns

Immediately correct or reject work that does any of the following:

- “Found service X in a menu, no `/service-x` URL, therefore gap.”
- Uses Google snippets alone to say a page does not exist.
- Targets a tiny subservice while a strong parent practice page exists.
- Says or implies only one service deserves its own page.
- Starts with SEO instead of client experience.
- Writes the email before evidence notes.
- Starts searching for email before the website opportunity passes validation.
- Accepts `info@`, `contact@`, reception, office or admin inboxes.
- Guesses an email address based on a domain pattern.
- Uses generic praise as personalization.
- Pushes for a call in the first email.
- Makes ranking or indexing guarantees.

# Session Rules

At the start of each session:

1. Read `VALIDATION_RUNNER_LAST_UPDATE.md`.
2. Resume from the exact next lead / batch stated there.
3. Do not re-run already finalized leads unless the last update explicitly marks them for review.
4. Preserve all previous evidence and status.
5. If the live site changed materially, note the change instead of silently overwriting the old conclusion.

At the end of each session:

1. Update `VALIDATION_RUNNER_LAST_UPDATE.md`.
2. Record counts and exact stopping point.
3. Record unresolved Review leads.
4. Record any recurring patterns or new edge cases.
5. Record any changes to the SOP/rules.
6. Record the exact next action for the next runner.
7. Never leave the handoff file vague.

---

# Core Philosophy

> Different legal services bring different clients with different questions, emotions, priorities and expectations. Dedicated service pages let each service have the right conversation with the client looking for it.

A dedicated page is not valuable only because it has a separate URL. It can have its own message, structure, imagery, emphasis, CTA and tone while staying inside the firm’s overall brand.

The selected service is the **best first demonstration** of that structural idea.

The lead is not outreach-ready until there is a **direct public email for the actual decision maker**.
